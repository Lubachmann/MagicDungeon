# Long-term progression direction

MagicDungeon should support a short visit with a clear goal and sustained play through changing builds, dungeon decisions and gradually unlocked challenges. These are design goals, not a claim that the current build already provides a particular number of hours of content.

## Current playable loop

Choose a crafted loadout, enter a dungeon, earn temporary levels and skills, find blueprints and resources, return voluntarily or on death, and use everything kept at the smithy. Equipment slots retain their upgrades across equipment choices. A player can track a discovered equipment recipe or next slot upgrade; its requirements and material sources appear before a run and in the journal. Daily-run resources do not count toward permanent goals.

## Principles

- Every expedition should advance at least one player-chosen goal, even after a loss. Preserve the established rule that discoveries and materials are kept on death.
- A short session should have an achievable next step, while deeper exploration offers additional choices and rewards.
- Gear, skills and enemies should create different tactical interactions, not merely larger numerical requirements.
- Long-term goals must remain understandable: explain sources, requirements and probabilities before resources are spent.
- Do not rely on forced wait timers, unavoidable losses, paid boosts or excessive inventory management to extend play.
- Challenges must remain meaningful for different loadouts; no progression system should be assumed balanced until tested.

## Next work, in order

1. Playtest a new character from first expedition through crafting and equipping several items. Measure actual time to first craft, blueprint duplication, the danger of each floor, and whether tracked sources are easy to reach. Record issues before changing balance.
2. Give equipment more distinctive identities: weapon-specific effects and defensive tradeoffs with clear tooltips, not only the same critical/speed/vampirism bonus applied to every item. Keep each build viable, and give players enough information to compare choices.
3. Build reusable encounter and room variations with visible risk/reward choices. Avoid replacing one fixed route with mandatory farming of a single enemy. Expand targetable material sources while retaining readable enemy drop indicators.
4. Add milestone goals and unlocks beyond initial progression, tied to meaningful new enemy mechanics, optional difficulty and blueprint families. Check that old content has a purpose and that rewards do not require one optimal loadout.
5. After playtesting the core loop, extend the present five-level slot cap with deliberate costs, difficulty tuning and capped or diminishing scaling; then test higher-level and Endless balance. Do not promise hundreds of hours from a numerical cap increase alone.
6. Improve long-run readability and access: targetable blueprint sources, history and collection views, accessible controls and clear saved-run behavior. Review every experimental menu visually and verify controller navigation.

## Testing and release gates

Keep automated coverage for tracked goals, crafting, slot migration, dungeon rewards, death and extraction, save/continue, and daily-mode isolation. Run human playtests across fresh and advanced saves. Test progression and encounter balance across several build types and seeds; automated simulations can identify problems but cannot establish enjoyment or actual playtime. Existing release notes and legacy tests refer to older equipment rules, so maintain compatibility deliberately and update their expectations when behavior changes.
